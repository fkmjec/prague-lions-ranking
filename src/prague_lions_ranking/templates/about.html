PATRIC

Welcome to PATRIC: Player Assessment via TrueSkill: Rating of Impact & Consistency. This app is a tool for estimating skills of players repeatedly competing against each other in any team sport. PATRIC builds on Microsoft's TrueSkill algorithm. Unlike traditional 1v1 match-making algorithms like Elo, TrueSkill supports team-based games of any team size.

The basic intuition behind the ranking is that winning games improves your rating, losing games decreases it. The magnitude of the change depends on the difference of ratings in the two teams. Only the final result (win/loss/draw) matters, not the score itself.

About TrueSkill

Each player's performance is modelled as a Gaussian distribution with two parameters: μ (mu), your estimated average performance level, and σ (sigma), the uncertainty in that estimate. Winning moves μ up, losing moves it down. Bigger upsets (meaning a team with a much smaller rating than its opponent wins) cause larger shifts. New players start with high σ, which shrinks as they play more games.

A team's performance is the sum of its players' performances (a sum of Gaussians is itself a Gaussian). The displayed TrueSkill value is a conservative estimate computed as μ − 3σ, meaning your true skill is very likely above that value (see the three-sigma rule). Reducing σ by playing more games therefore improves your TrueSkill rating even without winning more.

TrueSkill Through Time (TTT)

The TTT variant, which is used in this project, improves on vanilla TrueSkill by propagating results both forward and backward through time via a Bayesian network, rather than only updating estimates after each result.

For example, if you beat someone early in the season who later becomes the best player on the team, TTT will credit you for that result retroactively - vanilla TrueSkill would only account for their rating at the time of the match, which could have been low.

Using TTT for Ultimate Frisbee was inspired by Jake Smart, who did this for the college team Brownian Motion.

Practical Notes

Weight. A match can have weight higher than 1, which means it will be entered that many times into the TrueSkill calculation algorithm. It still counts only as one match in relevant statistics in the App (games count, teammate stats, etc.). The purpose of this is to give more significance to longer matches over one round of mini games.

Only the result counts. The algorithm has no knowledge of the score, only the Win/Loss/Draw matters.

Smaller teams calibrate faster. In a minis game each player has a larger influence on the outcome, so the algorithm can extract more information about individual skill from each result. In a 7v7 game, the calibration is much slower.

Players' sigma (uncertainty) depends on the opponents' sigma. Facing a low-sigma player gives the algorithm an accurate reference point, so your own sigma decreases more after the game (regardless of the result). Facing a new, high sigma player, provides less information, so the effect on your own sigma is lower. This also means the algorithm works best when the pool of players is not constantly changing.

Early losses can still raise your TrueSkill. True skill is calculated as mu minus 3 sigma. When a player is new, sigma is large, keeping your visible rating low. Even a loss forces the uncertainty down, and so even a loss can improve the overall TrueSkill value early on (it is much less likely to happen after some calibration).

Disclaimer

Luck and many other factors will always play a role in team sport and the algorithm and app are admittedly imperfect, so PATRIC is more an effort for gamification and rising the stakes than a super-accurate estimation of how good players actually are.
